The Art of Strumming
Building Rhythm from the Ground Up
The Fundamentals

Strumming is the heartbeat of rhythm guitar. Before learning complex patterns, master the core motions that make every great rhythm player sound locked-in and musical.

Hand Position & Pick Hold
  • 1 Relax your grip. Hold the pick between thumb and index finger — firm enough not to drop it, loose enough to flex. Tension is the enemy of groove.
  • 2 Pivot from the wrist. Your strumming motion originates at the wrist, not the elbow. Think of it as shaking water off your fingertips.
  • 3 Keep the arm moving. Even on rests, let your arm continue its down-up motion — simply lift the pick off the strings. This is called "ghost strumming" and it locks your internal rhythm.
  • 4 Angle the pick slightly. A slight tilt (10–15°) lets the pick glide across strings smoothly instead of catching and stuttering.
Todd's Tip ♩
"Count out loud every time you practice. Your brain can't fully focus on both playing and counting silently — speaking the beat out loud forces the two hemispheres to cooperate. It feels awkward at first. That's exactly the point."

Pattern 1 — All Downs
Du Du Du Du
Quarter-note downstrokes only. Your first pattern — build confidence and even tone.
Pattern 2 — Down / Down-Up
Du DU DU DU
Downstroke on beat 1, then down-up on beats 2, 3 & 4. Introduces upstrokes.
Pattern 3 — The Classic Folk
Du DU dU DU
Beat 3 downstroke is a ghost — arm keeps moving but doesn't hit the strings.
Pattern 4 — The Pop Standard
Du dU dU DU
Heard in thousands of hit songs. Two ghost downstrokes create the signature feel.
Pattern 5 — The Reggae Skank
dU dU dU dU
Muted downstrokes on every beat, upstrokes on every "and." This is the signature "chick-a" sound of reggae rhythm.
Practice Rule ♩
Master each pattern at 60 BPM before increasing speed. Accuracy at slow tempo transfers directly to speed. Rushing is how bad habits get cemented.

Common Mistakes
  • ✕ Stopping when you make a mistake. Keep going — hesitation breaks the rhythm more than the wrong note does.
  • ✕ Gripping the pick too tight. Your tone will be harsh and your wrist will tire. Relax.
  • ✕ Practicing without a metronome. You cannot hear your own timing errors in real time. The metronome is non-negotiable.
  • ✕ Moving too fast too soon. Speed is earned, not assumed. Slow is smooth; smooth is fast.
